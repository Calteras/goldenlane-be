Differences from the plan as written:

- **Weights are applied when a score is computed, not stored on each event.** The plan said each event stores its weight at write time. Applying them at scoring time means a tuning change in `trust_config` re-scores the whole history, which is what shadow mode needs. Events keep their evidence (timestamps, `corroborated`, payment mode).
- **The SUSPENDED tier is never computed.** The highest a score can earn is RESTRICTED; only a person can suspend (S10).
- **No-show corroboration uses the platform's own clock** for when it saw `READY`; the vendor's claimed wait is kept as evidence next to it.
- **Late pickup** (a completed order waited for more than 30 minutes) is recorded as its own small event.

Scope: the customer platform (`streetfood-customer-api`, Go) as the owner of customer risk, the POS (`ansilum-be`) and its two vendor clients (`ansilum-web`, `ansilum-flutter`) as the place vendors see and act on it, and the customer app (`flutter_mvvm_riverpod`).

Goal: the system and the vendor both know when a customer is likely to abuse the platform, in a way that is proportionate, explainable, reversible and fair to honest customers and honest vendors.

Not legal advice. Section 9 lists what counsel must review (Indonesian PDP Law, UU 27/2022, consumer protection) before enforcement goes live.

## 1. What exists today (and the gaps)

Facts taken from the code, so the plan builds on them.

| Area | Today | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Order lifecycle | `UNPAID, CREATED, SUBMIT_FAILED, PENDING_ACCEPTANCE, ACCEPTED, PREPARING, READY, COMPLETED, REJECTED, CANCELLED, EXPIRED` (`internal/entity/order.go`) | No state for "vendor prepared it, customer never came". A READY order ends as `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` with a free-text `reason`. The platform cannot tell a no-show from any other cancel. |

| Reviews | `reviews`: one per order (`UNIQUE order_id`), only `COMPLETED` orders, rating 1 to 5, comment up to 500 chars | One direction only (customer rates vendor). No vendor-to-customer signal. No anomaly detection, no moderation, no hold queue. |
| Payments | Pay at pickup (default) or online. Refunds, a 15 min payment window, a fee ledger | No dispute or chargeback tracking, no velocity checks, no card-testing detection. |
| Identity | Firebase uid per sign-in method. `customers.phone` and `email` are optional | Google, Apple and email sign-up (added recently) make accounts cheap to create. `DeleteCustomer` wipes phone and name, but a new uid is a fresh account, so deleting resets any reputation. |
| Devices | `devices(fcm_token UNIQUE)`, a token moves to whoever registers it last | Used only for push. No device fingerprint, no attestation. |
| What the vendor sees | `platformCustomerId`, name and phone in the submitted order | No risk signal, no history, no way to refuse a customer or require prepayment. |
| Operator tooling | `/v1/admin` (merchants and gateways), one bearer token | No customer lookup, no restriction, no audit trail. |

The default payment is pay at pickup. That is where the vendor carries the loss, so no-show handling comes first.

## 2. Principles

1. **Signals, not verdicts.** One event never flags a person. Risk comes from patterns over time, with decay, so a bad week does not follow a customer forever.
2. **Fair to new users.** A new account is "unknown", not "risky". Scores are smoothed so one cancel on the first order does not tier a customer.
3. **Evidence over accusation.** Every negative event has a source (system timestamp, vendor action, payment gateway) and is stored with its evidence. Vendors can be wrong or dishonest, so vendor reports carry less weight than system facts, and are capped per vendor.
4. **Proportionate responses.** Friction first (confirm, prepay, lower limits), restriction next, suspension last and only with human review.
5. **Explainable.** Each decision lists the reasons in plain words. The customer sees them and can appeal. The vendor sees a label and facts, never the raw score or other vendors' names.
6. **Reversible.** Restrictions expire, scores decay, appeals can overturn. Every override is audited.
7. **Two-sided.** The same machinery protects customers from vendor abuse (false no-shows, retaliation) and vendors from customer abuse.
8. **Privacy by design.** Collect the least needed, hash identifiers used for linking, keep a retention schedule, give access and correction rights (section 9).
9. **Shadow first.** Every new rule runs in shadow mode (computed, logged, not enforced) before it can affect a customer. Every enforcement has a kill switch.

## 3. Abuse catalogue

Every case we can name, with what to watch and how to respond. "Sprint" says where it is first handled (section 8). The list is the checklist for review: add or strike items here before any sprint starts.

Response vocabulary: **log** (record only), **warn** (tell the customer), **friction** (extra step), **limit** (lower caps), **prepay** (online payment only), **hold** (queue for human review), **restrict** (block a capability), **suspend** (block the account), **tell vendor** (show a flag in the POS).

## 4. Layered architecture

```
 L0 Prevention      friction at request time (limits, verification, prepay, confirmations)
 L1 Event capture   append-only trust events with evidence (who, what, when, source)
 L2 Features        rolling counters and ratios per customer, per device, per vendor
 L3 Scoring         explainable rules with decay and smoothing, shadow mode first
 L4 Tiers           NEW / GOOD / WATCH / RESTRICTED / SUSPENDED (+ reasons)
 L5 Enforcement     policy table: tier x capability, with expiry and kill switches
 L6 Surfaces        customer notices and appeals, vendor badge and controls, operator console
 L7 Governance      audit log, overrides, retention, metrics, tuning loop
```

Where each layer lives:

- **Customer API (Go)** owns L1 to L5 and the customer-facing part of L6. Customer data is here, and the order, payment and review events are here.
- **POS (`ansilum-be`)** owns the vendor actions that create evidence (mark no-show, payment refused, abusive, pickup confirmed) and shows the vendor badge. It stores no score. It receives a label and facts with each order.
- **Operator tooling** is a new set of admin endpoints and a simple console (Go admin API first, web console later).
- **Customer app** shows warnings, restriction notices, reasons and the appeal flow.

### Event capture (L1)

An append-only table `customer_trust_events`. Every row has: customer id, event type, source (`SYSTEM`, `VENDOR`, `GATEWAY`, `OPERATOR`), order id and outlet id when relevant, a severity weight at time of writing, evidence JSON (timestamps, durations, reason code), and `created_at`. Events are never edited. A correction is a new reversing event.

Core event types: `ORDER_COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW`, `LATE_PICKUP`, `CANCEL_BEFORE_ACCEPT`, `CANCEL_AFTER_ACCEPT`, `PAYMENT_REFUSED`, `PAYMENT_EXPIRED`, `PRICE_CHANGED_REFUSAL`, `DISPUTE_OPENED`, `DISPUTE_LOST`, `REFUND_REQUESTED`, `REVIEW_HELD`, `REVIEW_ANOMALY`, `VENDOR_REPORT`, `DEVICE_LINKED`, `ATTESTATION_FAILED`, `RATE_LIMIT_HIT`, `APPEAL_UPHELD`, `APPEAL_DENIED`, `OPERATOR_OVERRIDE`. Positive events matter: completed pickups raise trust.

### Features (L2)

Computed from events, per customer, in windows (7, 30, 90 days): orders, completions, no-shows, cancels by stage, cancel ratio, refund ratio, payment failure ratio, rating distribution, distinct vendors, distinct devices, account age, verification level (phone verified, email verified, attested device). Also per device and per hashed phone, so risk can follow a person across accounts. Also per vendor (their no-show, cancel and reject rates) to catch vendor abuse (E1 to E3).

Computed on write for hot counters, with a nightly rebuild job as the safety net (same pattern as the existing workers). No new infrastructure.

### Scoring (L3)

Rule-based, deterministic, and explainable. Start with points, not machine learning.

- Each event type has a weight (config table, tunable without a deploy).
- Weights decay with a half-life (default 45 days for no-shows, shorter for minor events).
- Positive events (completed pickups) subtract.
- **Smoothing:** the score is computed against a prior of `k` neutral orders, so a customer with 1 order and 1 cancel is not 100 percent bad.
- **Per-vendor cap:** one vendor can contribute at most a set number of points in a window. This limits damage from a vendor who reports unfairly (E1, E5).
- **Corroboration:** vendor-sourced negatives count at full weight only if backed by system facts (the order really reached `READY`, the 20 minute grace period passed, and nobody looked the order up by its pickup code). Otherwise at reduced weight.
- Output: a score, a tier, and the top contributing reasons as readable codes.

Example starting weights (all configurable, review in S0):

| Event | Points | Half-life |
|---|---|---|
| No-show (pay at pickup, vendor confirmed, grace passed) | +30 | 60 d |
| No-show (prepaid) | +20 | 60 d |
| Cancel after accept | +10 | 30 d |
| Payment refused at pickup | +35 | 90 d |
| Payment window expired (online) | +4 | 14 d |
| Lost dispute or chargeback | +60 | 180 d |
| Early cancel before accept | +1 (only counted above a ratio) | 14 d |
| Held review confirmed abusive | +15 | 90 d |
| Completed pickup | -6 | 90 d |
| Verified phone | -10 (one-off) | none |

Example tiers (review in S0):

| Tier | Meaning | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| NEW | Fewer than 3 finished orders. Neutral. | default |
| GOOD | Reliable | score below 20 |
| WATCH | Pattern forming | 20 to 49 |
| RESTRICTED | Limited capabilities | 50 to 89 |
| SUSPENDED | Cannot order (human confirmed) | 90 or more, or operator action |

A single event can never move a customer from GOOD to SUSPENDED. SUSPENDED needs a human (or a confirmed fraud signal such as a lost dispute plus a linked banned account).

### Enforcement (L5)

A policy table maps tier to capabilities. Capabilities are things the system can already gate:

| Capability | NEW | GOOD | WATCH | RESTRICTED | SUSPENDED |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Max open orders (today 5) | 2 | 5 | 3 | 1 | 0 |
| Checkouts per hour (today 20) | 6 | 20 | 10 | 3 | 0 |
| Pay at pickup | only after phone verified | yes | yes, vendor may require prepay | no, online only | no |
| Online payment | yes | yes | yes | yes, with deposit rule | no |
| Reviews | yes | yes | held for check | held | no |
| Needs extra confirmation at checkout | no | no | yes (reminder of no-show policy) | yes | n/a |

Restrictions have an expiry and a reason, are stored in `customer_restrictions` (type, reason code, starts, ends, source, created by), and are checked in `Checkout` through one function. Kill switch per capability through config.

### Surfaces (L6)

**Customer.** Plain notices in the app: a friendly reminder after the first no-show ("the vendor prepared your order; please cancel early next time"), a warning before a limit, and a clear message with reasons when limited. A short appeal form. A "how to restore" explanation (finish N clean orders, or wait for decay). No scores shown, only reasons and what helps.

**Vendor.** Each order in the POS (web panel and Flutter) shows a label, never a score: `New customer`, `Reliable`, `Caution`, `Prepay required`. A tap shows facts that are safe to share: "2 no-shows in the last 30 days", "18 completed orders". No other vendors' names. Vendors can set an outlet policy: require prepayment for Caution, auto-reject Restricted, personal blocklist. Vendors can mark outcomes with structured reasons and report abuse.

**Operator.** Customer lookup, event timeline, current tier and reasons, active restrictions, linked accounts, override and appeal queue, audit log, per-vendor outlier report.

## 5. Contracts and data changes (summary)

Customer API (Go), over the sprints:

- `merchant_orders`: add `ready_at`, `no_show_at`, `cancelled_by` (`CUSTOMER|VENDOR|SYSTEM`), `close_reason_code`, `pickup_code`, `pickup_confirmed_at`, `pickup_confirmed_via` (`CODE|MANUAL`).
- New tables: `customer_trust_events`, `customer_trust_profile` (score, tier, counters, updated at), `customer_restrictions`, `customer_identifiers` (hashed phone, email, device ids with first and last seen), `trust_appeals`, `vendor_customer_blocks`, `customer_ratings` (vendor thumbs, with a reason the system fills in), `banned_identifiers` (permanent ban list, hashed), `customer_reports`, `review_moderation`, `trust_config` (weights, thresholds, policy).
- Order status stays as it is. Add `NO_SHOW` as an order outcome (status or reason code, decided in S0).
- New endpoints: customer-facing `GET /v1/me/trust` (notices and reasons only), `POST /v1/me/appeals`; admin endpoints for lookup, restriction and overrides.

POS contract (additions to `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT.md`):

- Structured `reasonCode` on cancel and complete: `NO_SHOW`, `CUSTOMER_REQUEST`, `OUT_OF_STOCK`, `VENDOR_CLOSED`, `CUSTOMER_UNREACHABLE`, `PAYMENT_REFUSED`, `ABUSIVE_CUSTOMER`, `VENDOR_NOT_AT_SPOT`, `OTHER`. Free text stays as a note.
- `order.status.changed` carries `reasonCode` and `evidence` (`readyAt`, `waitedMinutes`).
- The submitted order carries a `customerTrust` block: `{ label, completedOrders, recentNoShows, requirePrepay }`. Only vendor-safe facts.
- New POS actions: mark no-show (allowed only after `READY` plus a grace period, default 20 minutes), report customer, and confirm pickup with a code.

Pickup code (**in scope again**, decision 4): every order gets a short code the customer shows at the cart. Its main job is speed: in a queue the vendor types the code to find the order, instead of hunting for a name. Its second job is evidence: an order found and completed by code is `pickup_confirmed_via = CODE`, which disproves a false "I never got it" claim (A12) and a false no-show (E1), and makes a real no-show easier to prove. Completing without the code stays possible (`MANUAL`), so the code never blocks a sale in a rush. Details in Sprint 2B.

## 6. Identity linking without over-collecting

- Store hashed identifiers (salted HMAC, key in the secrets file like `ENCRYPTION_KEY`): phone, email, device id, payment instrument fingerprint from the gateway.
- Link accounts by shared hashed identifiers. A strong link (same phone plus same device) carries risk over. A weak link (same IP) is logged only.
- **Permanently banned customers** (decision 5): keep hashed phone, payment fingerprint and device identifiers with no expiry, after account deletion too, if counsel confirms the legal basis (section 9). An upheld appeal deletes them.
- **Everyone else:** on account deletion keep the hashed identifiers and risk summary only for the retention period (suggested 12 months), if counsel confirms. Otherwise delete them and accept a weaker defence against D2.
- Because email, Google and Apple sign-up are free accounts, require a **verified phone** before pay-at-pickup for NEW accounts. This is the cheapest strong control against D1 to D4.
- Device integrity: Android Play Integrity and iOS App Attest, used as one signal, never as the only reason to block.

## 7. Metrics and tuning

Track from the first sprint, in a simple table or dashboard:

- No-show rate (all orders, pay at pickup, prepaid), per vendor.
- Share of orders by tier, tier movement, time in tier.
- Restriction count, expiry count, appeal rate, appeal overturn rate (a high overturn rate means the rules are too strict).
- False positive proxy: customers restricted who then completed several clean orders.
- Vendor adoption: share of vendors using the badge, prepay requirement, blocklist.
- Vendor outliers: no-show and cancel rate z-scores.
- Review hold rate and release rate.
- Cost: refunds, chargebacks, gateway fees on cancelled paid orders.

Tune weights and thresholds from shadow-mode data (S3) before enforcing (S4). Revisit monthly. Only consider a model later, when there are enough labelled outcomes and an explainable fallback exists.

## 8. Sprints

One sprint is one unit of work and one prompt. Each is shippable alone, behind a flag, and leaves the system consistent. Do them in order, except where noted. Rough effort: S = 2 to 3 days, M = about a week, L = 1.5 to 2 weeks (one developer).

S0 decisions ─► S1 capture ─► S2 no-show flow ─► S2B pickup code ─► S3 scoring (shadow) ─► S4 enforcement

                                                         ├─► S5 vendor surfaces

### Sprint 2: The no-show flow end to end (M)

Goal: the case example works. Vendor prepared it, customer never came.

- POS web (`ansilum-web`, the new orders panel) and POS Flutter: a "Mark as no-show" action on `READY` orders, enabled after the grace period. Needs a confirm step and a reason (`NO_SHOW` or `VENDOR_NOT_AT_SPOT`).
- POS backend: new transition `READY` to `CANCELLED` with `reasonCode NO_SHOW`, evidence (`readyAt`, `waitedMinutes`), and no sale created. Also an optional auto-expiry of long-idle `READY` orders with `NO_SHOW_AUTO`, lower weight.
- Go: receive the event, write `NO_SHOW` (source `VENDOR`, corroborated by system timestamps), notify the customer with a friendly message. Prepaid no-shows are **not refunded automatically** (decided). The message says so plainly and tells the customer how to contact us to report the vendor or ask for a refund, which is handled by hand.
- The pickup code is a separate sprint (2B). The grace period is 20 minutes, read from `trust_config`.
- Customer app: notice screen and push text.
- Never count `VENDOR_NOT_AT_SPOT` against the customer (E4).

Acceptance: a no-show produces one event, one customer notice, correct payment outcome, and no sale in the POS dashboard. A vendor cannot mark no-show before the grace period or on a non-`READY` order. Marking twice is a no-op.

### Sprint 2B: Pickup code (S to M)

Goal: the customer shows a code, the vendor finds the order in seconds, and the platform gets proof of pickup.

- Go: generate a short code when an order is created (4 digits, unique among that outlet's **open** orders, retried on collision, reused once the order closes). Store it in `merchant_orders.pickup_code`. Send it to the POS in the submitted order (`pickupCode`). Return it in the customer's order.
- POS backend (`ansilum-be`): store it on the online order and add an exact lookup to the staff list (`GET /streetfood-orders?outletId=…&code=1234`). It must find the order among open and the last day's finished orders, scoped to the outlet and tenant.
- POS web (orders panel) and POS Flutter: a search box at the top of the orders list that takes the code (numeric keypad, finds the order on the fourth digit, no Enter needed). One tap on the result opens it.
- When the vendor finds an order by code and completes it, record `pickup_confirmed_via = CODE` and `pickup_confirmed_at`. Completing without a code is still allowed (`MANUAL`). It never blocks a sale.
- Customer app: the code is shown large on the order screen once the order is accepted, and repeated in the "ready" push. It works offline from the last loaded order.
- Evidence rules for the trust system: a completed order with `CODE` closes any "I never got it" claim. A no-show is stronger when nobody looked the order up by code during the wait. A vendor with a high `MANUAL` completion rate next to many "never got it" claims is a signal for vendor outlier review (E1).
- The code is a lookup handle, not a secret. Someone who sees it can claim the food, which is a low stakes risk, so it is stored in plain form (hashing four digits adds nothing) and is not shown in lists to other customers.

Acceptance: the vendor finds the right order by typing the code in under 3 seconds, a wrong code or another outlet's code finds nothing, two open orders at one outlet never share a code, and an order completed by code is marked as such.

Kickoff prompt: "Implement Sprint 2B: pickup code generation in the Go API, the POS lookup endpoint in `ansilum-be`, the search box in `ansilum-web` and `ansilum-flutter`, and the code display in `flutter_mvvm_riverpod`."

## 9. Privacy, legal and fairness checklist

For counsel and for the product owner. Do this in S0 and re-check before S4 and S8 go live.

- Lawful basis and purpose statement for processing risk data, in the privacy policy and at sign-up (UU 27/2022 PDP).
- Notice to customers that a trust score exists, what it affects, and how to contest it.
- Right to access and to correct: `GET /v1/me/trust` and the appeal path.
- Automated decisions: no suspension without human review. Restrictions are limited and reversible.
- Retention schedule: events (for example 13 months), hashed identifiers of non-banned customers after account deletion (12 months, legal basis to confirm), hashed identifiers of permanently banned customers (no automatic expiry, reviewed yearly, legal basis to confirm), appeals and audit (longer).
- Permanent ban: the justification, the severe-violation criteria, the human confirmation and the appeal route must be written in the terms and the privacy policy before S8 ships.
- Data minimisation: no device-level precise location, only hashed identifiers, no social data.
- Vendor access: label and safe facts only. Vendors are bound by terms not to publish them.
- Bias check: review tier distributions by area and by vendor for unintended skew before enforcement.
- Security: access to the admin endpoints is role-based and logged, hashing keys in the secrets file, never in `.env` in git.
- Consumer protection: the rules page is clear, in Indonesian, and a restricted customer is always told how to restore access.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| False no-shows from vendors punish good customers | Grace period, corroboration, per-vendor cap, outlier discounting, pickup code evidence, appeals, customer notices on the first event |
| Vendors use the badge to discriminate | Label only, no score, no cross-vendor names, outlier review (E3), terms |
| Over-blocking drives honest customers away | Shadow mode first, NEW is neutral, gradual rollout, overturn-rate metric |
| Abusers adapt (new accounts, devices) | Identity linking, phone gate, attestation, rate limits, monthly review |
| Score becomes a black box | Rules only, reasons stored with every decision, config in a table |
| Data breach of risk data | Hashing, least privilege, audit, retention |
| Permanent ban wrongly applied (shared phone, stolen identity, vendor lies) | Severe criteria only, two humans confirm, appeal, yearly review, strong identifiers only (phone, payment, device), email never alone |
| Vendors use the new customer rating for revenge | Thumbs with reason codes, once per order, corroboration, per-vendor cap, outlier discounting, operator report |
| Complexity in `Checkout` hot path | One policy function with a cache (same idea as the account cache in `identity`), p95 budget set in S4 |
| POS and platform versions drift | Optional fields with defaults (`OTHER`), contract tests, deploy order: platform first |

### What decision 5 means in practice

**Vendor rating of customers.** Decided: thumbs only, and it must be near zero effort because vendors are busy in a rush. The design rules:

- **One tap, no form, no typing.** A thumbs up or thumbs down on the order card or in the completion toast. Nothing is required, and skipping is the default and counts as neutral.
- **The system fills in the reason.** The vendor never picks a reason from a list. The reason code comes from what happened: a no-show action is already a thumbs down with reason `NO_SHOW`, a refused payment is a thumbs down with `PAYMENT_REFUSED`, a completed order that the vendor taps thumbs up on is `GOOD_CUSTOMER`. An optional reason chip appears only after a thumbs down on an order with no system fact, and it can be ignored.
- **Never blocks the work.** No modal, no confirmation. The control sits on the finished order, can be tapped later (up to 7 days) and disappears on its own.
- **One tap on the same screen the vendor already uses**: the orders panel in the POS web, the order screen in the POS Flutter app.
- Each rating is a `VENDOR_REPORT` event with the per-vendor cap and the corroboration rule from section 4. A thumbs down with no system fact (order never reached `READY`, no refused payment, no no-show action) counts at reduced weight. A thumbs up counts as a small positive. Only customers who ordered from that vendor can be rated, once per order.

**Permanent ban that survives a new email.** Email is the weakest identifier because anyone can make a new one, and since Google, Apple and email sign-up are free, the ban must not depend on it. The ban is therefore keyed on identifiers that are costly to replace, in this order of strength:

1. verified phone number (hashed),
2. payment instrument fingerprint from the gateway (hashed),
3. device identifier plus attestation (hashed),
4. email only as a weak, supporting link.

How it works: when an operator confirms a **severe violation** (see below), the customer's account is suspended and the strong identifiers go on a `banned_identifiers` list with no expiry. Any new account that verifies a banned phone, pays with a banned instrument, or comes from a banned device is blocked at sign-in or at first order with the neutral message "this account cannot use the app". Because pay-at-pickup requires a verified phone (decision 3), a banned person has to find a new phone number and a new device to return.

What counts as severe, so the permanent ban stays rare and defensible: a lost chargeback or confirmed payment fraud, confirmed threats or harassment toward a vendor, confirmed multi-account evasion of an earlier ban, or repeated confirmed no-shows after warnings and a restriction (**decided: 5 corroborated no-shows within 90 days, after at least one restriction**). Ordinary cancels, late pickups and one or two no-shows never lead to a permanent ban. They lead to the graduated tiers in section 4.

Safeguards that stay in place even though the ban is indefinite:

- A human operator confirms every permanent ban, with a second approver (section 4 roles). No ban is automatic.
- The customer is told the reason category and can appeal. An upheld appeal removes the identifiers from the list.
- "Indefinite" means no automatic expiry, not "cannot be reviewed". The operator console lists bans for periodic review (suggested every 12 months).
- Only hashed identifiers are kept, never raw phone numbers or device data, and only for people on the ban list.
- This needs counsel's confirmation under the PDP Law before S8 ships (section 9). If counsel limits the retention period, the ban becomes long-lived instead of indefinite, and the rest of the design is unchanged.

## 12. Suggested first release

If only four sprints can ship first: **S1 (capture), S2 (no-show flow), S2B (pickup code), S3 (shadow scoring)**. They answer the case example, give vendors a real way to record it, and give real data to set the thresholds. Enforcement (S4) then ships on evidence rather than guesses, followed by the vendor badge and rating (S5) and the phone gate from S8, which is the cheapest strong control now that sign-up is free and also what makes the permanent ban hold.
